Privacy policy

Last updated 15 November 2026

Hook is an early-access desktop app for working with AI. This policy covers hookcode.io — the landing page, the early-access waitlist, desktop sign-in, and the model relay the app uses — and nothing else. Your conversations themselves stay on your computer.

What we collect

If you join the waitlist, you sign in with Google and we store:

  • the verified email address on your Google Account;
  • a stable Google account identifier (the OpenID sub value), so that joining twice does not create two entries;
  • your name, if Google provides it;
  • the interests you select and your preference for a future Hook plan, including its displayed price and billing period;
  • your onboarding selections and changes as you make them, even if you leave before finishing;
  • the date and time you joined, your current onboarding step, completion time if you finish, and your waitlist status.

We do not receive your Google password, your contacts, your files, or any other data from your Google Account beyond the identifier, email, and optional name described above. We record your onboarding choices directly to understand what people want from Hook. Apart from the Meta advertising pixel described below, we do not use third-party analytics scripts.

The desktop app and model requests

Hook signs you in with Google through a short device code. The app stores an opaque access token on your computer and sends it with model requests. Only a hash of that token is kept on our server, and it stops working when you sign out, when your access is revoked, or when you ask us to delete your account.

Your conversations and pictures are stored locally by the app. When you send a message or a coding agent makes a model request, the request is sent to our relay at hookcode.io and forwarded to our model provider. We do not store the text, pictures, tool definitions, or model replies. We do store the metadata needed to run and measure the service:

  • your account identifier and the request time;
  • a chat identifier and, for coding work, a run identifier, so usage can be grouped by conversation;
  • input and output token counts, cached and uncached input tokens, reasoning tokens, duration, and the HTTP status;
  • the selected model name and whether the request came from chat or a coding agent.

This metadata is used to understand how efficiently Hook uses models and to operate access controls. It is not used for advertising and is not sold. The model provider receives the request content under its own privacy policy.

When an agent uses web search, the search query is sent to DeepSeek's search service and the results are returned to the agent. We do not store the query or its results. Server logs contain only the account identifier, the result count and the time taken.

Advertising measurement

We advertise Hook on Meta (Facebook and Instagram) and use the Meta Pixel (pixel ID 1156718026779225) to measure how those ads perform. The pixel tells Meta that a page was viewed or that someone signed up after seeing an ad, so we can see which ads bring people to Hook and stop spending on ones that do not.

The pixel sends Meta the page address, the event that happened, and technical information about your browser and device. It can also read and set the _fbp and _fbc cookies described below, which help Meta recognise the same browser — for example, after you click one of our ads. We do not send Meta your name or email address from this site.

Meta processes this information under its own privacy policy. You can limit ad personalisation in your Meta ad settings or block tracking scripts in your browser.

Cookies

The site sets these first-party cookies:

  • hook_flow — lives for 10 minutes and protects the Google sign-in round trip against forgery;
  • hook_joined — lives for 7 days and associates your onboarding progress with your Google sign-in;
  • _fbp — set by the Meta Pixel, lasts up to 90 days, and identifies this browser for ad measurement;
  • _fbc — set by the Meta Pixel when you arrive from one of our Meta ads, lasts up to 90 days, and records which ad you clicked.

hook_flow and hook_joined are HttpOnly, Secure, and SameSite=Lax. The Meta cookies are written on our domain by Meta's script and are not read by Hook. The site sets no third-party cookies for any other purpose.

Your browser also uses session storage to remember your onboarding choices while you move between screens. This stores your selected interests, plan preference, and onboarding step, and is cleared when your browser session ends. Choices that have not yet reached our server are also kept in local storage, associated with your Google account identifier, so they can be retried when you return with the same sign-in. They are removed after a successful save.

Why we keep it

Your email address is used for exactly one purpose: to tell you when early access to Hook opens, and to send the few messages that come with it (invite, and anything that changes the invite). We do not sell or rent it and we do not add you to any other list. To measure our ads we share limited page-view and signup events with Meta as described above; we never send Meta your email address or name from this site.

Your interests, plan preference, and partial onboarding progress help us understand what early members want to create with Hook and where they stop during signup. Choosing a plan records a preference and does not create a paid subscription. Token and cache statistics help us see how efficiently the app and its coding agents use the model, so we can improve them.

Where it lives

The waitlist, account status, access tokens and usage metadata are stored in a single SQLite database on a server we operate. Model requests are relayed to our provider, currently DeepSeek; its handling of request content is governed by its own policy. Google's sign-in itself is covered by Google's privacy policy. The Meta Pixel is operated by Meta, and the information it collects from this site is covered by Meta's privacy policy.

How long we keep it

Until early access ends or you ask us to remove it — whichever comes first. When the waitlist is no longer needed, the database is deleted. Usage metadata is kept for the same period so that long-term efficiency can be compared.

Removing your data

Email nsgi3111@gmail.com from the address you signed up with and we will delete your waitlist entry, revoke your app token, and remove your usage metadata. You can also sign out in Hook, which revokes that token immediately.

Your rights

You can ask us what we hold about you, ask for a copy, ask for a correction, or ask us to delete it. Write to the address above and we will answer within 30 days.

Children

Hook is not intended for anyone under 16, and we do not knowingly collect their data.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. Material changes will be announced to the address you signed up with.
